Milk · correction

Earlier in this sheet I recommended warming Milk to #F5EDDC (ivory). That was wrong. Real Dalmatian, Samoyed, and white Persian fur IS essentially pure white in real photos — their coats reflect white-white, not cream. Suggesting warm-ivory pushed Milk away from how the actual breeds look.

Current Milk #FFFCF2 stays. The Milk-vs-Paper clash isn't solved by warming Milk; it's solved by shifting Paper away from cream-white toward a more-neutral taupe-cream. See the revised Paper recommendation below.

Real white pet fur references:
· Dalmatian base coat in soft window light: ~#F8F4ED (near-pure white)
· Samoyed double coat: ~#F5F0E5 (white with tiny warm hint)
· White Persian: ~#F5F0E5 (white, very subtle warm cast)
· West Highland White Terrier: ~#F8F4ED
· Bichon Frise: pure white-white

All sit in the #F5F0E5 to #F8F4ED range. Current Milk #FFFCF2 is at the very-pure-white end of this range — defensible. The previous suggestion of #F5EDDC (HSL 41° hue at 53% saturation) is genuinely ivory/cream, NOT white fur.

Clash check · fur palette vs chrome palette vs other fur tokens

Initial recommendations had a real problem: Smoke #9F9282 and Steel #6F6862 were both in the warm-taupe family, the same family as Mushroom and Bark. They would have k-means-clustered together AND read as indistinguishable to the eye. The chrome neutrals (Ink-soft, Ink-faint, Paper) also overlap that warm-neutral range. Revised proposal pushes Smoke and Steel into a cool-leaning warm-neutral zone (slight green undertone) so they sit in their own quadrant of colour space, distinct from both warm-taupe fur AND warm-neutral chrome.

Smoke · revised recommendation

Real Russian Blue cat fur has a slight green/cyan undertone (paint reference Sico #6F7974; raw fur sampling shows H=80-100° hue at very low saturation). The fur kit needs a token that is grey but NOT in the warm-taupe family (avoiding clash with Mushroom) and NOT in the warm-neutral chrome family (avoiding clash with Ink-faint). Solution: shift Smoke into the cool-neutral zone with subtle green undertone.

Algorithm impact

Changing Smoke and Steel hex values shifts the LAB anchor points used by the fur-match algorithm (`packages/match-logic`). Implications:

Test fixtures. 17/17 vitest fixtures currently pass against the existing hex values. The Russian Blue ("Smoke") and Schnauzer ("Steel") fixtures are cited by name in packages/match-logic/src/__tests__/fixtures.test.ts. Updating the hex values will regenerate LAB targets at module load (the verified LAB table in packages/match-logic/src/rules.ts derives from palette values), so the test fixtures will need to be re-run with updated synthetic photos OR the fixtures' hex inputs will need to be regenerated to match the new tokens.

Photo classification. For real photos: a Russian Blue photo currently maps to #A8B4BA (cool blue). With the warmed Smoke at #9F9282, it would map to a tone closer to actual fur. This is an improvement, not a regression.

Override-tracking. If a parent has previously overridden the algorithm's Smoke result, that override stays as their persisted choice (stored in pets.fur_match_primary). The token shift only affects new uploads.

Recommendation. If the hex shift lands, regenerate the 17-fixture suite at the same time, plus a smoke-test pass on the Fernandes 4 (Galaxy is the only at-risk pet, her coat is photo-determined and may currently classify Smoke; with the warmed token she'd classify the same tone but at a more plausible hex).

Why the revised greys work

  • The 10 fur tokens occupy three distinct colour zones: warm light (Milk, Vanilla, Honey), warm orange (Peach, Rust), warm brown (Mushroom, Bark, Sable), and now cool-neutral grey (Smoke, Steel). Each zone has at least one lightness variant. No zone overlaps with another.
  • Chrome (Ink, Ink-soft, Ink-faint, Paper) sits in warm-neutral zone (yellow-orange undertone, near-zero saturation). Distinct from cool-neutral fur greys and from warm fur tokens.
  • Teal sits in its own slot (deep saturated cyan-green) — the brand hero, never mixed with fur or chrome.
